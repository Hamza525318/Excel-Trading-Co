Split Rivet Nut

Blind Split Rivet Nut  ·  Four Legged Rivet Nut  ·  M6 & M8  ·  Mild Steel

The split rivet nut, also called a blind split rivet nut, four pronged rivet nut or four legged rivet nut, is a blind threaded insert with a body slit into four legs. When it is set with a rivet nut tool, the legs fold outwards behind the panel like the petals of a flower, gripping a large area of the back face. That wide footprint gives high pull-out strength and stops the nut spinning, even in plastics, fibreglass, plywood and other soft or brittle materials where a standard rivet nut would crush the hole or pull through. Installed from one side only, it gives a strong, reusable metal thread. Excel Trading Corporation keeps M6 and M8 in mild steel in stock.

How It Works

1

Drill the Hole

Drill a clean hole to suit the body of the split rivet nut, through the panel or into the hollow section.

2

Set with the Tool

Thread the nut onto the M6 or M8 mandrel of a rivet nut tool, push it into the hole and pull. The four legs fold out behind the panel.

3

Bolt Up

Unscrew the mandrel. The panel now has a strong, reusable metal thread, installed entirely from one side.

After setting: the four legs spread out behind the panel and clamp it against the flat head.

Why Choose a Split Rivet Nut?

Large load footprint

The four legs spread the load over a much wider area behind the panel than a standard rivet nut, so soft and brittle materials are not crushed or cracked.

High pull-out strength

With the legs locked behind the panel, the nut resists being pulled through thin sheet, plastics and composite panels under load.

Resists spinning

The folded legs grip the back of the panel, so the nut does not turn in the hole when the bolt is tightened or removed.

Forgiving fit

Works across uneven or thicker panels and slightly oversize holes, where a standard rivet nut may not set properly.

Installation Tips

  • ▸ Drill a clean, round hole. In fibreglass and plastic, use a sharp drill at low speed to avoid chipping the edge.
  • ▸ Screw the nut fully onto the mandrel so every thread is engaged before setting, otherwise the thread can strip.
  • ▸ Hold the tool square to the panel so all four legs open evenly behind it.
  • ▸ Make sure there is enough clear space behind the panel for the legs to fold out.
  • ▸ Do not over-tighten the bolt in soft materials. Tighten until the joint is firm.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a split rivet nut?

A split rivet nut, also called a blind split rivet nut, four pronged rivet nut or four legged rivet nut, is a blind threaded insert whose body is slit lengthwise into four legs. It is pushed into a drilled hole and set with a rivet nut tool from one side only. As it is set, the four legs fold outwards behind the panel and clamp it against the flat head, leaving a strong, reusable metal thread. Because the legs grip a large area behind the panel, split rivet nuts are used in plastics, fibreglass, plywood and other soft or brittle materials where a standard rivet nut could crush the hole or pull through.

What is the difference between a split rivet nut and a standard rivet nut?

A standard rivet nut forms a small bulge behind the panel as its body collapses, which works well in metal sheet of the right thickness. A split rivet nut has a body cut into four legs that fold right out behind the panel, giving a much larger footprint. That spreads the load over a wider area, so it gives higher pull-out strength and less damage in soft or brittle materials such as plastics, fibreglass and plywood, and it tolerates uneven panels and slightly oversize holes. For metal sheet of normal thickness, a standard round or hex body rivet nut is usually the better and more economical choice.

Which materials can split rivet nuts be used in?

Split rivet nuts are made for materials that are too soft or brittle for a standard rivet nut, including plastics, fibreglass (GRP) and other composites, plywood and board panels, and thin sheet metal. They are also used in hollow sections such as tubes and box sections, and in panels with uneven surfaces. Because the four legs spread the clamping load behind the panel, they are less likely to crack brittle materials or pull through soft ones. For very soft wood, check the joint with a trial installation, as the grip depends on the density of the board.

What tool do I need to install a split rivet nut?

Split rivet nuts are installed with a standard rivet nut tool, either a hand tool or a pneumatic or battery tool, fitted with the matching M6 or M8 mandrel and nosepiece. The nut is screwed fully onto the mandrel, pushed into the hole and set by pulling the tool, which folds the four legs out behind the panel. The mandrel is then unscrewed. No special tool is needed, so workshops that already set rivet nuts can use split rivet nuts straight away. Excel Trading Corporation also supplies rivet nut tools.

Do split rivet nuts spin in the hole?

Split rivet nuts resist spinning well. Once set, the four legs press against the back of the panel and clamp it firmly against the flat head, so the nut does not turn when a bolt is tightened or removed. This is a common problem with round body rivet nuts in soft materials such as plastic and fibreglass, and it is one of the main reasons buyers choose split rivet nuts for these materials. Avoid over-tightening the bolt in very soft panels, as excessive torque can still crush the material.

Can a split rivet nut be used in a blind or closed section?

Yes. A split rivet nut is installed entirely from one side, so it is ideal for closed sections, tubes, box sections and panels where the back cannot be reached. The only requirement is enough clear space behind the panel for the four legs to fold out. Before installing in a narrow section, check that nothing behind the hole, such as a cable, pipe or second wall, will block the legs.
