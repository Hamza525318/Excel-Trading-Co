Frequently Asked Questions

What is a blind rivet?

A blind rivet, often called a pop rivet, is a fastener installed from one side of the work. It is inserted into a drilled hole and set with a rivet tool, which pulls the mandrel so the rivet body expands behind the material and clamps the parts together. Blind rivets are used wherever the back of the joint cannot be reached, such as in tubes, boxes, panels and enclosures.

Which type of blind rivet should I choose?

Open end rivets suit general sheet metal work. Sealed (closed end) rivets help seal the joint against water and dust. Peel and bulb tite rivets spread the load on soft or brittle materials. Multi grip rivets cover a wide range of thicknesses with one part. Structural, Anlock and U-Bolt rivets are high strength rivets with a locked mandrel for vibration-prone and load-bearing joints.

Which materials are blind rivets available in?

Blind rivets are available in aluminium, steel and stainless steel bodies with steel or stainless mandrels, depending on the type. Match the rivet material to the parts being joined to avoid galvanic corrosion, for example stainless rivets for stainless sheet.
