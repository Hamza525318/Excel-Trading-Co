Frequently Asked Questions

What is a rivet nut?

A rivet nut, also called a blind rivet nut, nutsert or rivnut, is a threaded insert that is installed from one side of a panel to give thin sheet metal, tubes or plastics a strong, reusable internal thread. It is pushed into a drilled hole and set with a rivet nut tool, which pulls the threaded end so the body collapses and grips the back of the panel. Rivet nuts are used wherever a nut cannot be held behind the panel, such as in closed sections, enclosures, vehicle bodies and HVAC ducting.

Which type of rivet nut should I choose?

Choose a round body rivet nut for general sheet metal work in round holes. Choose a half hexagon or full hexagon body rivet nut when the joint will see higher tightening torque, because the hexagonal body sits in a hexagonal hole and resists spinning, with the full hex body giving the most resistance. Choose a split rivet nut, with four legs that fold out behind the panel, for plastics, fibreglass, plywood and other soft or brittle materials where a wide grip and high pull-out strength are needed.

What is the difference between a split rivet nut and a standard rivet nut?

A standard rivet nut forms a small collar behind the panel as its body collapses, which works well in metal sheet of the right thickness. A split rivet nut has a body cut into four legs that fold right out behind the panel, giving a much larger footprint and higher pull-out strength in soft or brittle materials such as plastics, fibreglass and plywood.
