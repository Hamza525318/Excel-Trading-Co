Tanged Free-Running Wire Threaded Insert

Wire Threaded Inserts for OEM Production

Light alloys such as aluminium and magnesium save weight, but a thread tapped straight into them wears, galls and strips under repeated assembly. A wire threaded insert solves this by placing a hard, smooth stainless steel thread between the bolt and the parent metal. The coil spreads the load over a larger area, so the joint is stronger and the thread lasts for the life of the part.

We supply the tanged free-running type in Metric, UNC, UNF, BSP, BSW and BSF threads, in 1D to 3D lengths, in SS 304 and Nitronic 60. They are used across aerospace and defence, automotive and EV, electronics and industrial machinery, both in new production and to repair stripped threads without scrapping costly castings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are wire threaded inserts used for?

Wire threaded inserts are used to create strong, wear-resistant internal threads in soft or critical parent materials such as aluminium, magnesium, cast alloys and plastics, and to repair stripped or worn threads back to their original size. A precision stainless steel wire coil is wound into an STI-tapped hole, spreading the bolt load over a larger area and protecting the parent metal from wear and galling. OEMs in aerospace, defence, automotive, electronics and industrial machinery use them both in new production and for maintenance repair, because the repaired hole takes the same bolt size and no drawing change is needed.

Are wire threaded inserts, helical thread inserts and screw thread inserts the same thing?

Yes. Wire threaded insert, wire thread insert, helical thread insert, helical coil insert and screw thread insert (STI) are all names for the same product: a coil of diamond-profile stainless wire that forms a standard internal thread once installed. The STI name also gives its name to the special STI tap used to prepare the hole. Different industries and countries prefer different names, but the product, installation method and standards such as DIN 8140 for metric threads are the same.
