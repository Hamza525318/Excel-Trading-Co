Choosing the Right Washer

Plain and load-spreading washers increase the bearing area under a bolt head or nut. That lowers the surface pressure, so the clamped part is not crushed or marked, oversized or slotted holes are bridged, and the joint keeps its preload. They matter most on soft materials such as aluminium, plastics, wood and thin sheet.

Locking washers solve a different problem: bolts working loose under vibration and dynamic loads. Wedge-locking washers such as Nord-Lock use tension instead of friction to hold the bolt, which makes them far more reliable than spring or toothed washers on vibrating machinery, vehicles, rail, wind and mining equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a washer do in a bolted joint?

A washer sits under the bolt head or nut and spreads the clamping load over a larger area. This lowers the surface pressure on the clamped part, so soft materials such as aluminium, plastics and thin sheet are not crushed or marked, oversized or slotted holes are bridged, and the bolt keeps its preload. Locking washers add a second job: they stop the bolt or nut from rotating loose under vibration. Choosing the right washer therefore depends on whether the main problem is surface pressure, hole size or vibration.

What is a wedge-locking washer and how is it better than a spring washer?

A wedge-locking washer, such as Nord-Lock, is a pair of washers with cams on the inside faces and radial teeth on the outside faces. The cam angle is steeper than the thread pitch, so any attempt by the bolt to rotate loose makes the pair expand and increases the tension instead of releasing it. Spring and toothed washers rely on friction and spring force, which can be lost under vibration, and independent tests show they offer little protection against self-loosening. Wedge-locking washers are therefore used on vibrating machinery, vehicles, rail, wind and mining equipment where a loose bolt is not acceptable.
